# First, work out which kind of ban you have

People say "my WhatsApp is banned" and mean two quite different things. The fix is not the same, so check this before anything else.

| | Personal or WhatsApp Business app number | WhatsApp Business API account |
|---|---|---|
| Where you see it | A screen on your phone saying the number cannot use WhatsApp | An alert in WhatsApp Manager inside your Meta Business Portfolio, or a "restricted" or "disabled" status in your platform |
| Who decides | WhatsApp, usually after reports or automated spam detection | Meta, under the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy |
| How you appeal | The support or review option on the ban screen, plus an email to WhatsApp support | Request a review in Meta Business Support Home for the affected account |
| What gets looked at | Your behaviour on that number | Your opt-in proof, your templates and your message quality |

A restricted API account can often still receive messages but is limited in what it can send. A banned app number is usually blocked completely. Keep that difference in mind when you read the fixes below.

# Why WhatsApp bans business numbers

WhatsApp does not publish a single checklist that explains every ban, and it can act at its own discretion. What it does publish is its Business Messaging Policy, and the patterns below match what that policy names as problems. They are listed roughly in the order we would check them.

## 1. You messaged people who never opted in

This is the big one. The policy says businesses may message only people who gave their number and agreed to receive messages from them. A phone number you found, bought or copied from a visiting card is not consent.

The tricky part is that WhatsApp does not need to prove you had no permission. It only needs enough people to tap **Block** or **Report** after your message. Every one of those taps is a signal about you.

## 2. You sent bulk messages from the normal app or a browser extension

Many cheap "bulk sender" tools work by controlling WhatsApp Web or by scanning a QR code, then sending messages one after another from an ordinary number. To WhatsApp this looks like automation on a consumer account, and the policy lists unauthorized large-scale messaging through a service as grounds for action. It is the main reason these tools come with a ban warning in the fine print.

The risk grows with volume. Ten messages to friends is one thing. Two thousand messages in an afternoon to people who have never heard of you is another.

## 3. Too many people blocked or reported you

Quality is a running score. If a meaningful share of the people you message block or report you, WhatsApp may limit what you can send, and repeated problems can end in a ban. Blocks come from messages that feel irrelevant, too frequent, or hard to stop.

This is why a small, interested list often beats a large cold one. Fewer blocks keep your quality healthy, and healthy quality lets you send more later.

## 4. A new number suddenly sent a lot

A brand new number that goes from zero to thousands of messages in one day looks nothing like a normal business. Even on the official API, new accounts start with a daily limit that grows as you send good-quality messages. Meta moved these limits to the whole business portfolio in October 2025, so look at your own limit in WhatsApp Manager instead of trusting a number from a blog, including this one.

## 5. The same message, copied, with links in it

Identical text sent to a long list, especially with shortened links or heavy promotion, trips spam filters. Small changes help, such as the customer's first name and a line about what they actually did with you. A message that reads like it was written for one person is also less likely to be reported.

## 6. Prohibited products or misleading claims

The policy bars some categories outright, including things like firearms, tobacco, drugs and real-money gambling, and it restricts others. It also forbids misleading or deceptive messages and pretending to be another business. A message promising a guaranteed result, a fake discount or a fake urgent deadline can get you reported and reviewed.

## 7. A bought or scraped contact list

Lists sold online are the fastest way to a ban. The numbers are old, the people never agreed to anything, and many are simply wrong. If you want a list, build it, one honest sign-up at a time.

# Official API and unofficial tools: what the risk looks like

You do not need to be a technical person to see the difference. One route is built into the platform, the other is built around it.

| | Official WhatsApp Business API | Unofficial sender (extension, QR-scan tool) |
|---|---|---|
| Sending method | Meta's own API, through an approved provider | Automation of a normal WhatsApp account |
| First message to a customer | Approved template message | Free text, nothing reviewed |
| Consent | Expected, and part of the policy you agree to | Not checked by anyone |
| Daily limits | Clear, and visible in WhatsApp Manager | Hidden, and WhatsApp can cut you off at any point |
| Effect of a ban | Appeal through Meta Business Support | Number can be lost with little recourse |
| Cost | Per-message pricing plus the platform fee | Often a low monthly price, until the number is banned |

The official route costs more per message and asks you to follow rules. In return you get a channel that is allowed to exist. # What to do right now if your number is banned

Stay calm, and do these in order. Rushed mistakes such as creating five new numbers in one evening make things worse.

## If your app number is banned

1. **Stop sending from that number.** Do not try to work around the block with the same SIM on another phone.
2. **Read the exact message on screen.** Take a screenshot. It tells you whether the block is temporary or permanent.
3. **Use the support or review option on that screen.** Explain in two or three sentences that you run a real business and what you use the number for.
4. **Wait for a reply.** Resending the request again and again does not speed it up.
5. **Prepare a fallback.** Tell your regular customers on another channel that you may be slow to reply for a few days.

## If your API account is restricted or disabled

1. **Open WhatsApp Manager** in your Meta Business Portfolio and select the account. The policy alert there usually names the reason.
2. **Pause campaigns.** Do not send promotions until you understand the problem.
3. **Collect your proof of consent.** A sign-up form, a checkout checkbox, a click-to-WhatsApp ad or a chat where the customer asked for updates all count, as long as you can show when and how.
4. **Request a review** from Meta Business Support Home, choose the violation, add a short explanation and attach evidence.
5. **Track the case** in the In Review section and fix the cause while you wait.

# How to write a ban appeal that gets read

Reviewers see a lot of angry messages. A short, calm appeal with facts stands out. Here is a structure you can adapt. Write it in your own words, because copied appeals look copied.

> Hello, our WhatsApp number [number] was restricted on [date]. We are [business name], a [type of business] in [city]. We use this number to send order updates and offers to customers who signed up through [form, checkout, ads]. We have records of their opt-in. We have now paused promotional messages, added a clear STOP option and removed contacts who did not consent. Please review our account.

Notice what is missing: no blame, no capital letters, no threats. It says who you are, what the number does, what you changed, and what you want.

If the first review does not work, you can usually ask again after improving something real, such as your opt-in process. Sending the same text with no change rarely helps.

# How to avoid a ban in the first place

Most bans are preventable. The habits below take a few hours to set up and save you from a much longer recovery later.

## Collect opt-in properly

Give people a reason to say yes. Put a checkbox at checkout that says they want order and offer updates on WhatsApp. Add a click-to-WhatsApp button to your website with a [prefilled message](/blog/whatsapp-link-with-prefilled-message). Run a "message us to get the offer" ad. In each case you keep a record of the moment they agreed.

## Make stopping easy

End promotional messages with a plain line such as "Reply STOP to unsubscribe", and then honour it straight away. People who cannot leave will block you instead, and blocks hurt more than unsubscribes.

## Use approved templates for the first message

On the official API, a conversation that you start must begin with a template that Meta has approved. Write it clearly, give it the right category, and avoid the common rejection reasons covered in our guide to [why templates get rejected](/blog/why-whatsapp-templates-get-rejected). If you are unsure about the category, read [marketing or utility](/blog/marketing-or-utility-whatsapp-template) first.

## Warm up gradually

Do not send to your whole list on day one. A simple example plan, to be adjusted to your own limit:

| Day | Who to message | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Your most engaged customers, a few hundred at most | Builds a good quality record |
| 3 to 5 | Recent buyers and people who messaged you | Likely to reply, unlikely to report |
| 6 to 10 | The rest of your opted-in list in batches | Lets you spot problems early |
| After that | Raise volume only if blocks stay low | Quality, not speed, raises your limit |

## Write like a person

Use the customer's first name, mention what they did, and say why you are writing. Compare these two messages.

Weak: "BIG SALE!!! 50% OFF ALL ITEMS. CLICK NOW bit.ly/xyz"

Better: "Hi Priya, the kurta set you looked at last week is back in your size. We have kept it for you till Sunday. Reply YES and we will send the payment link."

The second one is shorter on hype and longer on relevance. It is also the kind of message people reply to instead of reporting.

## Watch your numbers every week

Check blocks, replies and delivery rates after each campaign. If blocks climb, pause, look at the message and look at the list, and change one thing at a time. Treat it like a health check, not an emergency.

## Keep a human way out

If you use a chatbot, give people a clear route to a real person. WhatsApp's policy expects automated experiences to offer a way to reach a human. A [free AI chatbot](/tools/ai-chatbot-widget) can handle first questions while a team member takes over the rest.

# Signs your account may be in trouble

Look out for these before a ban lands.

- A notice in WhatsApp Manager about a policy problem or a quality drop
- Messages that used to be delivered now failing more often
- A sudden jump in people replying "stop" or "who is this"
- Your daily sending limit not growing even though you send regularly
- Customers telling you they did not sign up for your messages

Any one of these is a reason to slow down and review your list and templates. It is much easier to fix a warning than a ban.

# FAQ

**Why is my watsapp business number banned?**

The most common reasons are messaging people who never opted in, using unofficial bulk sender tools, and getting blocked or reported by many recipients. WhatsApp can also act on messages that look misleading or promote restricted products. The notice on your screen or in WhatsApp Manager usually gives the reason.

**Can I get a banned WhatsApp number back?**

Sometimes. You can ask for a review, and a calm, factual request with proof of consent has the best chance. There is no guarantee, and repeat or serious violations can mean a permanent block. Plan a backup channel while you wait.

**Is bulk messaging allowed on WhatsApp?**

Yes, through the official WhatsApp Business API, to people who opted in, using approved templates. Bulk sending from a regular or Business app number through an unofficial tool is the risky part, because WhatsApp treats unauthorized large-scale messaging as a policy problem.

**How long does a WhatsApp ban appeal take?**

It varies. Some reviews finish within a few days and others take longer, and WhatsApp does not promise a timeline. Track the case in Meta Business Support for the API, and avoid sending the same appeal repeatedly.

**Does the official API remove the risk of a ban completely?**

No. It removes the biggest risk, which is using automation WhatsApp did not approve, but you can still be restricted for sending unwanted or misleading messages. Opt-in, relevant content and easy opt-out keep your account healthy.

**How do I stop customers from reporting my messages?**

Message only people who asked for it, send at sensible times and frequency, make each message relevant, and give a clear STOP option. Fewer unwanted messages means fewer blocks and reports, and that protects your quality rating.

**Should I buy a contact list to grow faster?**

No. Bought lists have no consent, many wrong numbers and a high chance of reports. A smaller list built from real sign-ups will outperform it and keep your number safe.
