# What a WhatsApp QR code is

A WhatsApp QR code is a picture that a phone camera can read. When someone scans it, their phone opens a WhatsApp chat with your business number, often with a first message already typed.

There is nothing special inside the code. It simply stores a normal WhatsApp link, the kind that starts with `wa.me`. That means you can put it anywhere a link would be awkward: posters, menus, packaging, shop windows and business cards.

# How to make one

1. **Build your link.** Add your number with the country code and write the first message people should send. The [free WhatsApp link generator](/tools/whatsapp-link-generator) does this and shows a QR code next to the link.
2. **Download the QR code** as an image.
3. **Scan it yourself** with a phone before you use it anywhere.
4. **Add it to your design** and print or publish it.

The WhatsApp Business app can also make a short link and a QR code for your number from its business settings. The menus change between versions, so look for the short link or QR code option in the app's business tools.

# Decide the message before you print

A QR code stores the link exactly as it was when you made it. If you later want a different opening message, or you change your number, the printed code will still point to the old one. Settle on the wording first, and keep a spare copy of the file so you can reprint.

If you expect to change things often, point the QR code at a short address on your own website that redirects to the WhatsApp link. You can then change where it leads without reprinting.

# Print it so it scans

Most failed scans come from the design, not the code. Keep to these rules:

- **Dark on light.** Use a dark code on a plain light background. Light-on-dark and low-contrast colours scan badly.
- **Keep the empty border.** Leave a clear margin around the code. Do not crop it or put text close to the edge.
- **Do not stretch it.** Resize with the proportions locked so it stays square.
- **Make it big enough.** A rule of thumb is a width of about one tenth of the distance someone will scan from. A code on a poster read from two metres away needs to be much larger than one on a business card.
- **Use a sharp image.** A blurry file prints with blurry squares. The downloaded PNG is large enough for most print jobs.

# Where it works well

| Place | Why it works |
|-------|--------------|
| Restaurant tables and menus | Customers ask for the bill or place an order without waving a waiter down |
| Shop windows | People who walk past after closing can still send a message |
| Packaging inserts | Buyers can reach you about a delivery or a return |
| Invoices and receipts | A direct line for questions about a payment |
| Event stands and flyers | Visitors can follow up when they are ready |

Add a short line next to the code, such as "Scan to chat with us on WhatsApp". People scan more when they know what will happen.
